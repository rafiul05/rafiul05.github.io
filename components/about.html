About me

Hello and welcome! I'm Mir Rafiul Ahmed, an applied economics graduate from the University of Maine. My research sits at the intersection of energy and environmental policy and development economics, using applied microeconometrics alongside survey and experimental methods.

At UMaine I worked in the Noblet PFAS Lab on experimental environmental economics — research on PFAS contamination and risk perception — and on market development for Maine's marine aquaculture sector. Before that I was a Senior Research Assistant at the South Asian Network on Economic Modeling (SANEM), where I contributed to projects with the United Nations Development Programs (UNDP) and nternational Labour Organization (ILO), including a study on Bangladesh's transition to clean energy.

I hold a B.S.S. in Economics (Magna cum Laude) from Shahjalal University of Science and Technology, where I also worked on field research on poverty and cognitive development. I'm interested in research that connects to policy, particularly where it affects low-income households and local economies.

I'm now based in Corvallis, Oregon, and a prospective Ph.D. student in Energy Economics/Policy for Fall 2027.

Outside academia, I founded Econ Insider, a student-run platform for economic discussion and research. I also enjoy data visualization and public speaking, and I follow football, travel, take photographs, and read fiction.

Feel free to explore my work or reach out if you'd like to connect.
