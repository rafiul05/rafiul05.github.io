I am a Research Assistant in the Noblet PFAS Lab at the School of Economics, University of Maine, where I study how households respond to per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) contamination with Dr. Caroline Noblet, and how U.S. consumers make seafood choices in a National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA)-funded project with Dr. Keith Evans and Dr. Noblet. I completed my M.S. in Economics, with a concentration in applied economics, at the University of Maine in 2026.

As an applied economist, I work across energy, behavioral and experimental, environmental, and development economics, with an emphasis on questions that inform public policy. My methods combine survey and experimental design, applied econometrics, cost-benefit analysis, and spatial analysis. I have used them in municipal energy planning in Maine, in risk-perception and consumer studies with state agencies, and, over two years at the South Asian Network on Economic Modeling (SANEM) in Dhaka, in projects with the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), the Asian Development Bank (ADB), the International Labour Organization (ILO), and the Tara Climate Foundation on trade, nutrition, financial inclusion, and Bangladesh’s clean energy transition. My work falls into three areas.

Research

  1. Environment and Energy

    Household exposure to emerging contaminants such as PFAS, energy affordability and the distributional costs of clean energy transitions, and how communities plan for efficiency and renewable power.

  2. Behavioral Economics

    How information, risk perception, and trust shape choices about food, health, and the environment, studied through survey experiments and stated-preference methods.

  3. Development Economics and Public Policy

    Energy security and access, financial inclusion, and human capital in low- and middle-income countries, and the evaluation of policies designed to improve them.

I am based in Corvallis, Oregon, and am applying to Ph.D. programs for Fall 2027.
